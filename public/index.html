      How the values and levels are worked out
      

        
    
          
  • The gathered max temperature and humidity are one model's values for the same hour, taken from the model that gives the highest humidex, so the heat indices describe the hotter scenario. Upstream rain is the highest of four forecast points: Tena, Jatunyacu, El Chaco and Loreto.
    • 
          
  • Heat indices use the maximum temperature and the humidity at that hour. Dew point: Magnus formula. Humidex: Environment Canada, T + 0.5555 × (vapour pressure in hPa − 10). Wet-bulb: Stull (2011), valid near sea-level pressure.
    • 
          
  • WBGT: when you enter a reported value it is used in the brief; otherwise the brief gives a calculated value. It uses the Liljegren et al. (2008) model, the method behind the US National Weather Service WBGT forecasts and wbgt.app: natural wet-bulb and black-globe temperatures from air temperature, humidity, station pressure (sea-level pressure brought to Coca's 250 m), wind (10 m wind reduced to 2 m) and solar radiation (sun position at Coca and the forecast cloud cover). It is worked out for each daytime hour from 06:00 to 18:00; the brief gives the hottest full-sun hour and the shade value at that hour. WBGT = 0.7 × natural wet-bulb + 0.2 × globe + 0.1 × air temperature. Heat-stress concern by WBGT in full sun: under 25°C low, 25 to 27.9 elevated, 28 to 30.9 high, 31 and over extreme. It is a model estimate, not a measurement: an exposed sandbar or a boat deck can run hotter.
    • 
          
  • Suggested levels. Rain, highest 24 h total among the models: under 22 mm low, 22 to 31 moderate, 32 to 60 high, over 60 very high (the Ecuadorian advisory scale). Lightning: none low, isolated moderate, scattered or likely high. Heat, by humidex: under 30 low, 30 to 39 moderate, 40 to 45 high, 46 and over very high. Wind, by gusts: under 30 km/h low, 30 to 49 moderate, 50 to 69 high, 70 and over very high; at least moderate when thunderstorms are forecast.
    • 
          
  • River level, from the Napo at Coca (H1135): under 4.5 m low, 4.5 to 6.0 m moderate, over 6.0 m high, and one step higher when the trend is "rising fast". With no level reading, the outlook sets it and it shows as unconfirmed when there is none.
    • 
          
  • River outlook suggestion: rise likely with 30 mm or more of upstream rain (observed or forecast), or a rising river with 10 mm or more; rise possible with 10 mm or more upstream, 30 mm or more at Coca, or a rising river. These triggers are provisional until more paired readings exist.
    • 
          
  • Values gathered online (forecast task, INAMHI stations, advisories) are shown read-only with an "online" tag and replace anything typed. A field becomes editable only when nothing was gathered for it. Your judgement stays editable: river outlook, hazard levels, wording, advisory check, observed conditions note and the model table.
    • 
          
  • INAMHI stations: read from the INAMHI Visor by this site's server each time the page opens, every 15 minutes while it stays open, and when you press Refresh: levels at Napo at Coca town, Coca at San Sebastián and the headwater stations Quijos DJ Oyacachi and Salado AJ Coca; rain at the three CCS stations, Sierrazul and Puyo (listed in the Napo River section), plus Coca town for observed rain. The headwater levels are shown for context: in the 2 to 11 October record they did not anticipate the rises at San Sebastián. They fill the river level, time, 24 h change, trend, San Sebastián level and observed rain, ahead of the forecast values. Evening rain of 8 mm or more in 12 hours at Puyo or Sierrazul now suggests "rise possible": twice in ten days it came 6 to 10 hours before a rise of about 0.5 m that no other gauge announced.
    • 
          
  • River outlook days run from 05:00 to 05:00 (Tuesday covers 05:00 Tuesday to 05:00 Wednesday), for the level range and for the rain, observed and forecast. The most likely level, the bold line, recedes more slowly than the low end and adds half of the rain-driven rise; on the 2 to 11 October record it was off by 0.19 m on average, against 0.31 m for simply keeping today's level. The 24 h values (rain, level change, station minimum and maximum) end at the time of the last refresh.
    • 
          
  • River outlook, 5 days (provisional): the low end follows recession only (about 0.3 m a day, slower towards 4.5 m; the day after a pulse 60% of the rise comes off). The high end adds a rise sized by each day's forecast rain, the largest of the upstream points and Coca town (under 5 mm +0.1 m, 5 to 10 mm +0.3, 10 to 20 mm +0.6, 20 to 35 mm +1.0, more +1.4), and stays at least 0.6 m above the low end because unannounced rises of about 0.5 m came twice in ten days. Tomorrow's range is the river forecast used everywhere in the assessment; each day's five ranges are kept in the record, and the Record table compares tomorrow's range with the next day's level.
    • 
          
  • Trend: read it from the last 3 hours on the Visor chart. Rising is 0.03 m per hour or more (about 0.1 m in 3 hours); rising fast is 0.1 m per hour or more, which in this record marked the two rises of about 1 m.
    • 
          
  • The 24 h river change fills itself from the previous day's level when you leave it blank.
    • 
          
  • Export PDF produces an A4 report of the day on screen: hazard levels, a table of the numbers, then the brief text. Emoji are left out of the PDF because its built-in fonts have none. A draft is marked as a draft.
    • 
          
  • The issue time fills itself with the time you open the page (Ecuador time). Type over it to change it; it is fixed when you mark the brief as issued.
    • 
          
  • Every level and every line of wording can be overridden. Values tagged "auto" came from the morning gathering; "edited" are yours.
    • 
          
  • Gathered sources, read each time the page opens and when you press Refresh: MET Norway (ECMWF-based) hourly forecasts at Coca and the four upstream points; Open-Meteo for the model comparison (ECMWF, GFS, UKMO, ICON) and gusts; 7Timer (GFS-based) for instability; Coca airport METAR; GEOGLOWS modelled flow. Official advisories are not searched automatically in this version: check INAMHI and SNGR and tick the box. The day's brief is shared: entries, edits and Issue appear for everyone using this site within about 30 seconds, and if two people change the same field the last save wins. Live readings are read by each device; the launch day and header settings stay in this browser; each day's river forecast is kept for everyone.
